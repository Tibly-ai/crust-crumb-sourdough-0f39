The Slow-Living Morning Way

Thirty-six hours of patient fermentation: flour, water, salt, and time.

We believe that beautiful bread cannot be rushed. It must be coaxed into existence by cool morning air, mineral-rich mountain water, and wild cultures that have been breathing with us for generations.

At Crust & Crumb Sourdough, our day begins while the stars are still out. We don’t use commercial additives, industrial yeasts, or conditioning agents. Our process relies entirely on the natural rhythm of our mother starter, a wild culture nurtured daily with stoneground grain.

Each loaf is mixed by hand, folded in deliberate intervals, and allowed to rest in woven proofing baskets for up to thirty-six hours. This cold, patient fermentation naturally breaks down complex starches, resulting in a blistered, caramelized crust, an open custard-like crumb, and a gentle acidity that is easy on the body.

The Pillars of Our Craft

No short cuts, no compromises

Every loaf tells a story of local agriculture, patient traditional methods, and micro-organisms native to our Pacific Northwest environment.

The Origin
The mother starter

Our starter is a living community of wild yeasts and friendly lactic bacteria. Kept active by continuous feeds of stoneground dark rye and spring water, it has been the soul of our sourdough for years.

Every loaf is naturally leavened solely by this wild culture. Because of this, our bread develops dynamic organic acids that impart a unique, rustic complexity.

The Earth
Heritage flour & grains

We source single-origin heirloom wheat varieties from small-scale regional farms. These grains are stone-ground to preserve the nutrient-rich germ and bran, resulting in flour that is fragrant and deeply nourishing.

By avoiding processed white roller-milled flours, we support regenerative local soil systems while serving bread with true grain character.

The Fire
A high-heat stone hearth

Our massive stone hearth oven holds intense heat and dry steam, essential for producing that iconic deep blistered crust. When the wet dough hits the hot stone, it experiences a dramatic oven spring.

This rapid heat caramelizes the grain sugars and locks in moisture, resulting in the textural play between a thick, crunchy outer crust and a soft interior.
